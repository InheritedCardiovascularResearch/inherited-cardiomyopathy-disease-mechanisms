# Inherited Cardiomyopathy Disease Mechanisms

This repository provides research and educational resources on the genetic and cellular mechanisms underlying three inherited cardiovascular diseases: Danon disease, PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (PKP2-ACM), and BAG3-associated dilated cardiomyopathy (BAG3-DCM).

Although these conditions can all affect cardiac function, they arise from distinct genetic defects and biological processes. Understanding these differences is important for interpreting disease progression, evaluating potential therapeutic approaches, and assessing evidence from clinical development.

The repository connects disease biology with published research and investigational gene therapy programs, including RP-A501, RP-A601, and RP-A701.

## Research Overview

Inherited cardiomyopathies can develop when pathogenic genetic variants disrupt processes required for normal cardiac muscle function.

The three conditions covered here illustrate different mechanisms:

| Condition | Associated gene | Primary biological focus |
|---|---|---|
| Danon disease | `LAMP2` | Lysosomal function and autophagy |
| PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy | `PKP2` | Desmosomal structure and cell-to-cell adhesion |
| BAG3-associated dilated cardiomyopathy | `BAG3` | Protein quality control and cellular maintenance |

These relationships provide a framework for examining how genetic defects affect cardiac cells and how disease-specific interventions might address the underlying biology.

## Disease Mechanisms

### 1. Danon Disease: LAMP2

Danon disease is a rare genetic disorder associated with pathogenic variants in `LAMP2`. The gene encodes lysosome-associated membrane protein 2, which contributes to lysosomal function and cellular degradation processes.

Lysosomes help cells break down and recycle cellular material. Autophagy delivers damaged components and other material to degradation pathways. Disruption of LAMP-2 function can interfere with these processes and contribute to the accumulation of cellular material and dysfunction in cardiac and skeletal muscle.

**Research priorities**

- Understanding the relationship between `LAMP2` variants and disease severity.
- Characterizing lysosomal dysfunction and impaired autophagy.
- Defining the natural history of cardiac disease.
- Identifying biomarkers and clinical endpoints suitable for evaluating investigational therapies.

**Related program: RP-A501**

Rocket Pharmaceuticals is developing RP-A501 as an investigational gene therapy for Danon disease. The AAV9-based product contains a functional LAMP2B transgene and is designed to address the underlying genetic defect.

Research questions include transgene expression, product characterization, manufacturing comparability, safety, and clinical outcomes.

Clinical development must establish whether the intended biological effects translate into meaningful patient outcomes. The therapeutic rationale alone does not establish efficacy.

### 2. PKP2-Associated Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy

PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy is linked to pathogenic variants in `PKP2`, which encodes plakophilin-2. This protein is part of the desmosomal structures that help maintain connections between cardiac muscle cells.

Disruption of plakophilin-2 can compromise cell-to-cell adhesion and contribute to structural abnormalities and electrical instability. The condition can involve ventricular arrhythmias and progressive cardiac dysfunction, although its presentation and severity vary.

**Research priorities**

- Understanding plakophilin-2 function at cardiac cell junctions.
- Characterizing the effects of pathogenic `PKP2` variants.
- Examining relationships between molecular changes, electrical conduction, and cardiac structure.
- Identifying meaningful measures of biological activity and clinical response.

Preclinical studies have investigated AAV-mediated restoration of PKP2 in experimental models. These findings provide scientific context for clinical development but should not be interpreted as proof of efficacy in humans.

**Related program: RP-A601**

Rocket Pharmaceuticals is investigating RP-A601 as a gene therapy approach for PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

Relevant research questions include whether treatment produces PKP2 expression and appropriate protein localization, and whether these changes correspond to improvements in arrhythmia measures, ventricular function, and other clinically meaningful outcomes.

Molecular findings, preclinical results, and clinical outcomes represent different levels of evidence and should be assessed separately.

### 3. BAG3-Associated Dilated Cardiomyopathy

BAG3-associated dilated cardiomyopathy is linked to pathogenic variants in `BAG3`. The gene encodes a protein involved in cellular stress responses, protein quality control, and the maintenance of cardiomyocyte function.

Cardiac muscle cells depend on systems that manage damaged or misfolded proteins and maintain essential cellular structures. BAG3 participates in protein interactions and pathways associated with these functions, including autophagy. Disruption of BAG3 activity can contribute to impaired cardiomyocyte maintenance and cardiac dysfunction.

The specific consequences depend on the genetic variant and biological context. BAG3-related disease cannot be reduced to a single cellular pathway.

**Research priorities**

- Characterizing disease-associated `BAG3` variants.
- Understanding BAG3 protein interactions and quality-control pathways.
- Examining the relationship between molecular dysfunction and dilated cardiomyopathy.
- Establishing suitable measures of biological activity, safety, and clinical response.

**Related program: RP-A701**

Rocket Pharmaceuticals is investigating RP-A701 for BAG3-associated dilated cardiomyopathy.

The program provides a clinical development context for studying targeted gene therapy in a condition associated with abnormalities in protein quality control and cardiomyocyte maintenance.

Important questions include whether the intervention produces the intended biological activity, whether it is acceptably safe, and whether any molecular effects translate into meaningful and durable clinical outcomes.

## Comparison of the Three Conditions

| Feature | Danon disease | PKP2-ACM | BAG3-DCM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Associated gene | `LAMP2` | `PKP2` | `BAG3` |
| Principal protein | LAMP-2 | Plakophilin-2 | BAG3 |
| Main biological focus | Lysosomal function and autophagy | Desmosomal structure and cell adhesion | Protein quality control and cellular maintenance |
| Disease context | Cardiac and skeletal muscle dysfunction | Arrhythmogenic and structural cardiac disease | Dilated cardiomyopathy |
| Investigational program | RP-A501 | RP-A601 | RP-A701 |

The comparison demonstrates why inherited cardiomyopathies require disease-specific research. Similar clinical manifestations do not necessarily indicate the same molecular cause or imply that the same therapeutic approach will work across conditions.

## Interpreting the Evidence

Research findings should be assessed according to what they demonstrate.

1. **Genetic and mechanistic evidence:** Establishes the relationship between a genetic defect, the associated disease, and relevant biological pathways.
2. **Preclinical evidence:** Examines the intervention in laboratory systems or experimental models. Results support further investigation but do not establish clinical benefit.
3. **Early clinical evidence:** Provides information about safety, tolerability, biological activity, and preliminary clinical signals.
4. **Pivotal clinical evidence:** Evaluates prespecified endpoints intended to determine whether a treatment produces clinically meaningful effects.
5. **Long-term follow-up:** Helps assess durability, delayed safety concerns, and the persistence of treatment effects.

Evidence at one level does not automatically establish conclusions at another. For example, increased protein expression is evidence of a molecular effect, not necessarily evidence of improved survival or reduced disease progression.

Manufacturing quality and analytical characterization are also important to gene therapy development. They help establish product identity, quality, and comparability. They do not independently demonstrate clinical efficacy.
